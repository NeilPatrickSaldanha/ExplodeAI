# Video 0: What is ExplodeAI? (series opener and app tour), Simplified Technical English version

Written 2026-11-09. This is the script in `VIDEO_0_SCRIPT.md`, rewritten in the style of ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English). Rules applied: short sentences (about 20 words or fewer), one idea in each sentence, active voice, present tense, no contractions, no idioms, one word for one meaning, and the same name for the same thing every time.

**Limits of this version.** I wrote it from the STE writing rules. I did not check it against the official STE dictionary or with an STE checker tool, so some words may not be approved words. Run it through a checker if you need a formal STE claim. The cue lines in brackets are for you, not for speaking.

Target length: about 5 to 6 minutes.

---

## Script

**[0:00 Hook. Screen: Lesson 03 diagram. Move the Explode slider from 0 to 100%.]**

This is a sentence. An AI model changes it into numbers. Here, the machine comes apart in front of you, and you can see each part. This is ExplodeAI. In this video, I show you what it is and how to use it.

**[0:20 Who and why. Screen: your face, or the home page.]**

My name is Neil. I build software that connects AI to real systems. I learn better when I see a thing work. Long text is hard for me to read. So I made a place where you can take AI systems apart. You can change them, break them on purpose, and put them together again.

I made ExplodeAI with Claude Code. I decided what to teach, what to build, and how good it must be. I also make sure that I understand everything that I show. I do not hide this.

**[1:00 Home page and sidebar. Screen: home page, then the lesson list.]**

There are five lessons. They are RAG, tokenization, embeddings, attention, and agents that use tools. The lessons are in the list on the left. A tick shows when you look at all the stages of a lesson. Each lesson takes about 25 to 30 minutes.

**[1:20 The words switch. Screen: the Plain words and Technical names switch.]**

This switch is at the top. It is for people who do not know AI yet. Plain words is the default. It uses simple names for each part. Click Technical names to see the standard terms. The facts are the same in both.

**[1:45 The big picture. Screen: open the big picture bar.]**

Each lesson starts with the big picture. It shows the whole system in a few sentences. It gives one comparison for each part. It also gives one example that you follow through all the stages.

**[2:05 The diagram. Screen: the diagram, the slider, and the buttons below it.]**

This is the diagram. Each block is one stage. The slider moves the parts apart. The buttons below the diagram do these things. Flow shows what moves from one stage to the next. Data shows the real values from your last run. Isolate shows one part alone. You can choose 3D or 2D. Calm reduces the motion. [OPTIONAL: Move lets you move the diagram.]

**[2:40 A stage. Screen: click "Meaning reader". Scroll the stage panel.]**

Click a part to open its panel. The panel shows what goes in and what comes out. It tells a short story in plain words. It shows what the stage does to the example, with real numbers. It gives a comparison, and it also says where the comparison is not true. The box "Words you will hear" gives you the technical terms, one stage at a time. At the end, a question lets you check yourself. Try to answer it before you show the answer.

**[3:20 What breaks. Screen: "What breaks if removed?" and then the source-backed facts.]**

One feature is very useful. It is "What breaks if removed". Click it, and the lesson removes that stage. Then it runs the system again without the stage. You see what the stage is for. Below this, the facts have links to their sources.

**[3:50 Five modes. Screen: the tabs Explore, Tour, Experiment, Break and fix, Rebuild.]**

There are five modes. In Explore, you click the parts. In Tour, the lesson shows each stage in order. In Experiment, you change settings on a real run. The lesson tells you what to try and what to look for. In Break and fix, the system is broken, and you find the cause. In Rebuild, you put the stages in an order that works.

**[4:40 The tutor. Screen: the Ask ExplodeAI button.]**

The Ask ExplodeAI helper answers from the lessons. If the lessons do not cover a question, the helper says so.

**[4:55 Honesty. Screen: the Real execution and Simulation badges, and the Verified badge.]**

Each part has a label. The label says if the part is a real run, a simulation, or only a picture. The data is not real. It is made up. It does not make a claim about the world. Each fact has a link to its source. Each fact had a three-step check. I do not say that the content is perfect. This is why the sources are there.

**[5:20 Series plan. Screen: the lesson list.]**

The next videos each teach one topic in depth. The topics are RAG, tokenization, embeddings, attention, and agents. If you have a question, or if you see an error, tell me.

**[5:40 Close.]**

[If the website is public:] You can use ExplodeAI at the link below. The next video shows how an AI looks up information before it answers.

## Be ready to explain
- Why Plain words is the default, and what the other mode does.
- "What breaks if removed" is a real run of the experiment without that stage. It is not an animation.
- The documents, sentences, shop, and orders are made up. The attention lesson uses random numbers. The agents lesson uses a simple rule, not a real AI model.
- The "Verified" badge shows that the claims had the three-step check of the project. It does not show that everything is proven correct.
- How much you built with Claude Code, and what you did yourself.

## Do not say
"Guaranteed correct". "Learn two or three times faster". Numbers of users. Anything about the optional paid AI features.
